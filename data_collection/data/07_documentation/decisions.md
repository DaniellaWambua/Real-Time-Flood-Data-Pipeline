# Decisions

## D1, 2 November 2026, catchment design
Three exclusive sub-catchments. Mathare, Ngong (the full area above the Ngong outlet, which includes Motoine) and the Nairobi River (excluding Mathare and Ngong). Each outlet is delineated and the upstream areas are subtracted so the areas do not overlap. Reason, flood labels are attached to one area each, and nested catchments would count an upstream flood in every downstream area. Motoine was merged into Ngong because it had no flood episode with three or more independent reports, so it could not be evaluated alone.

## D2, 2 November 2026, scope and period
Nairobi only. Tana, Nzoia and Nyando are not part of this rebuild. Weather and static data are collected for 2004 to 2025. Flood labels are used from 2015 to 2025, because news-derived evidence is sparse before 2015 and an absence of reports then does not mean an absence of floods. Earlier EM-DAT events stay in the register as county-level context.

## D3, 2 November 2026, outlet placement
Outlets were placed using the upstream-area profile along each mapped river (profile_rivers.py), which shows where each tributary joins its main river. Mathare is placed at longitude 36.880 and Ngong at 36.941, 1 to 2 km above their junctions. The Nairobi River outlet is 500 m downstream of the Ngong junction. Every outlet is snapped to the highest flow accumulation within 3 cells, about 90 m. Reason, at a junction the main river has the largest accumulation, so an outlet too close to it captures the whole main river catchment.

## D4 Labelling and splits
Days are labelled flood, unknown or no_flood. Unknown days are excluded from training and testing negatives. Splits must keep every event_id inside one fold, and evaluation is walk-forward by year so that no future data informs earlier predictions. Labels cover 2015 to 2025 only because Groundsource is sparse before 2015.

## D5 Splits
Final test is 2024 to 2025 and is evaluated once. Development uses forward-chaining folds over 2015 to 2023 (validate 2019, 2021 to 2022, 2023). Flood days are few (194 in total, 87 in the test years), so results are reported pooled with uncertainty, and per-catchment results only for the test years. Precision is compared with each fold base rate.

## D8, 3 November 2026, comparison protocol
Models compared are the 3-day rainfall rule, random forest, gradient boosting, ExtraTrees and the LSTM, for today and tomorrow targets. The LSTM has three variants, class weights with raw rainfall (lstm_raw), class weights with log rainfall (lstm_log), and balanced mini-batches with raw rainfall (lstm_batches, train_lstm.py batches mode). Balanced batches keep every non-flood day in every epoch, so they are not undersampling. Class weights or balanced batches only, no undersampling, and no logistic regression. The primary comparisons are boosting against the rule and lstm_raw against the rule, on average precision and event recall at a 5 percent alarm share. All other models and metrics are secondary. Tree hyperparameters are fixed as in baselines.py and baselines_balanced.py and not tuned. ExtraTrees uses the forest settings. The LSTM uses the train_lstm.py network and training, 15 epochs, with positive weight equal to the square root of the class ratio in weights mode. Random models use seeds 0 to 2 and metrics are averaged over seeds. The alarm rule follows event_eval.py, the top 3, 5 or 10 percent of scored days in a fold, and an event counts as detected when an alarm falls in its catchment within 1 day. For the tomorrow target, events and alarms are matched on the day being predicted. Pooled development average precision is computed on the three folds concatenated, and the mean over folds is also reported because fold models are calibrated differently. Pooled top 5 percent recall and event scores sum hits over folds, with alarm shares set per fold. Intervals are 95 percent block bootstrap over catchment-months, 1000 resamples, paired across models. Reason, 194 flood days and 17 development events are too few for single-number comparisons.

## D6 Label gap years
2017 has no records in either source and 2020 has one EM-DAT record but no Groundsource episodes. Groundsource coverage grows strongly over time (3 episodes in 2015, 17 in 2024), so early no_flood days are less reliable. All no_flood days in 2017 and 2020 are set to unknown.

## D7 Balancing test
Undersampling non-flood days to 1:1 and 5:1 (training folds only, 3 seeds) was compared with class weights on the development folds. It gave no consistent gain for the forest, boosting or the LSTM, and often lowered average precision. Class weights were kept.

## D9, 3 November 2026, final test
Settings in D8 were frozen and confirmed before the final test. lstm_batches was added as a secondary variant before the freeze. The test (train 2015 to 2023, score 2024 to 2025) was run once with final_trees.py and train_lstm_final.py, which refuse to run again once their output exists. Nothing was tuned after the test. Average precision on the test is reported with lift over base rate, because the test base rate (0.042) is about 3.5 times the development rate (0.012). A fixed-threshold version of the rule (95th percentile of rain_sum_3d on 2015 to 2023) was added as a sensitivity check only. The model ranking differed between development and test, so neither ranking is treated as settled.

## D10, 3 November 2026, tuning run after the final test
After D9, hyperparameters were searched on the development folds only, as a separate post-hoc study. The D9 results stay the primary results. The search used about 30 settings each for forest, ExtraTrees and boosting, 14 per LSTM variant (units, layers, dropout, learning rate, epochs, window of 7, 14 or 30 days, batch size, and for weights mode the class-weight power of 0.5 or 1), and the single rainfall feature used by the rule. The frozen D8 setting is always setting 00. Every setting was scored on mean-of-folds average precision, pooled event recall at 5 percent and pooled top-5-percent recall, for both targets, with 3 seeds. The setting chosen per model is at least as good as setting 00 on all six numbers with the best mean rank. If no setting qualifies, the best mean rank is chosen and the trade-off is reported. The chosen settings were then scored once on 2024 to 2025. The 2024 to 2025 period had already been seen, so these test numbers are a second look and not an unseen test. The frozen rule_rain_3d stays the reference baseline, and the tuned rule is reported as rule_tuned. Class weights or balanced batches only, as in D8. Outcome: forest, ExtraTrees, lstm_raw and lstm_log met the all-six condition. Boosting and lstm_batches did not and took the best mean rank, and the rule kept rain_sum_3d. Development gains mostly did not carry over: tuned test AP was lower than frozen for ExtraTrees, lstm_raw and lstm_log, and higher for forest and boosting. Best fixed-threshold test F1 was 0.39 (frozen 0.40). D9 stays the primary result.

## D11, 3 November 2026, label audit design

The error analysis suggested that part of the low precision and recall comes from label noise. This audit measures that noise using independent evidence and does not use model output. Days come from three groups. Group A is every flood day. Group B is every no_flood day within one day of a one or two report Groundsource cluster or a city-wide report in the same catchment. Group C is a random sample of no_flood days, stratified by 3-day CHIRPS rainfall (under 10, 10 to 25, 25 to 40 and 40 mm or more) and by period (2015 to 2019 and 2020 to 2025), with a fixed seed. Selection reads only the features table, the event register and the cluster files, and never any score or prediction file.

Each selected day is checked against sources independent of Groundsource, such as ReliefWeb, Red Cross and IFRC reports, EM-DAT, DesInventar and named news reports not already in the cluster. A flood is confirmed when one such source places flooding in the catchment within one day. A no_flood label cannot be proven, so a day with no evidence after a recorded search is marked no evidence found and keeps its label. Every change is written to label_corrections.csv with the old and new status, the source, the checker and the date, and the daily table is rebuilt from the original register plus that file. The original files are not edited. Results are reported with both label sets side by side, and any threshold or model choice is made on development folds only.

D11 clarification, catchments. A city-wide report counts as being in the same catchment as a no_flood day when its footprint overlaps that catchment at all, so a large report covers all three catchments and a boundary report covers those it touches. The audit uses the three catchments of the daily table (Nairobi, Mathare and Ngong), with Motoine inside Ngong as in D1. A report is verified once, and that result applies to every catchment it covers.
